About Me

I'm a Presidential Scholar and Honors Program student at Alfred University, Class of 2029, studying Materials Science and Engineering with a minor in Computer Science. My path here ran through two Tanzanian secondary schools, a college-access program that changed how I saw my future, classrooms where I tutored younger students, and a community that helped fund my flight across the world.

Today I simulate metallic glasses on high-performance computing clusters, support students as a Resident Assistant, and help lead several campus organizations — while trying to open the same doors for others that were opened for me.

Alongside my major, I'm teaching myself software development. Computing already powers my research, and I want to understand the technology behind it. This website is one of my first projects.

  6. April 16, 2025

    Raising Awareness of Education Opportunities Abroad

    Role: Volunteer Speaker & Peer Mentor

    Mbezi Garden Hall, Mbezi, Dar es Salaam · Organized by Eng. Norbert Safari

    Having learned how to navigate the path to studying abroad, I volunteered to share that knowledge with secondary school students at an awareness event at Mbezi Garden Hall in Dar es Salaam. The event was organized by Eng. Norbert Safari, one of the most impactful parents at Feza Boys. Many talented students never apply abroad simply because no one has shown them how, so I walked them through the three things I wish I had understood sooner:

    • Scholarships and financial aid. Universities abroad offer merit scholarships and need-based aid that can cover much of the cost of study. I explained how to find these opportunities, how aid packages work, and that you can ask a university to reconsider an offer.
    • Standardized testing. Many universities ask for tests such as the SAT. I explained what these tests are, why they matter in an application, and how to plan time to prepare for them alongside regular studies.
    • The application process. I described each step: researching and choosing universities, writing personal essays that tell your own story, gathering documents and recommendations, and meeting deadlines. I also stressed staying persistent if it doesn't work out the first time, as it didn't for me.

    My goal was simple: make sure more Tanzanian students know these opportunities exist and believe they can reach them.

  7. 2025

    After months of essays, tests, and applications, I opened my decisions surrounded by friends — and was admitted to Alfred University as a Presidential Scholar. After I asked the university to reconsider my aid, my award was raised to a $54,000 scholarship package.

  8. May – June 2025

Family, friends, and my community came together and raised around $3,000, which covered every one of these costs.

Computational Materials Science Research

Undergraduate Research Assistant | Summer Undergraduate Research Fellow (SURF)

Dr. Zachary Knepp's Lab · Jan. 2026 – Present

  • Simulate the manufacturing of metallic glasses toward ultra-high-strength, lightweight, corrosion-resistant structural materials.
  • Run orbital-free DFT (OF-DFT) molecular dynamics simulations with DFTpy on the San Diego Expanse HPC cluster.
  • Fabricated a stable Al-Mg (67/33%) metallic glass via ultra-fast quenching at 1011 K/s.
  • Analyze phase transitions and atomic packing in OVITO; automate data extraction with Python.
  • Presented my poster, “Modeling Metallic Glass with Orbital-Free Density Functional Theory (OF-DFT)”, at the summer 2026 research poster session.

Featured poster: Modeling Metallic Glass with OF-DFT

Metallic glasses have no long-range atomic order, which makes them strong, corrosion-resistant, and conductive. Conventional Kohn-Sham DFT gets too expensive to model them at realistic sizes because its cost grows as O(N³). Orbital-free DFT grows only as O(N log N), so it can reach system sizes that conventional DFT cannot.

  • System: a 216-atom Al-Mg model (67:33, near the eutectic), built from a 6×6×6 fcc aluminum supercell with 33% of sites substituted by magnesium.
  • Method: OF-DFT molecular dynamics in DFTpy using the Wang-Teter kinetic energy functional, run on SDSC's Expanse supercomputer. The structure was melted and held at 1,100 K, then quenched to 300 K with a stepped ramp and a continuous linear ramp.
  • Analysis: structures visualized in OVITO; radial distribution functions, g(r), computed with my own Python script.
  • Result: the continuous quench produced an amorphous glass with no long-range order. Its broad, split g(r) peaks match real amorphous Al-Mg alloys.
  • Next: extract the glass transition temperature, benchmark against KS-DFT, and scale to larger supercells and new compositions.
